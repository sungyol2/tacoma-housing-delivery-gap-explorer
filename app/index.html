About this explorer

This self-directed portfolio project connects current zoning, parcel conditions, three for-sale and rental test prototypes, assessed-value acquisition benchmarks, and observed permit activity.

Financial values remain illustrative pending market validation. The interface deliberately exposes split zoning, overlays, confidence, and known limitations.

 Methods

How the screen works

1. Parcel and zoning base

Pierce County parcel parts are dissolved to a normalized parcel ID. Every parcel–zoning intersection is measured; the dominant zone is displayed while material split zones remain in the capacity calculation.

2. Candidate scope

The model starts with UR1, UR2, and UR3 base parcels. Existing residential and clearly developable vacant uses are retained. Parks, open space, schools, cemeteries, rights-of-way, utilities, drainage facilities, and golf courses are excluded. Ambiguous nonresidential uses require manual review.

3. Capacity and physical fit

Baseline density uses 1,500 sq ft/unit in UR1, 1,000 in UR2, and 750 in UR3, with area-weighted treatment of split zones. Before prototype fit, mapped 40%+ slopes, wetlands, biodiversity areas, special flood hazard areas, and protected-water buffers are removed. Each prototype then applies its own unit count, gross area, residual site-area, FAR, and parcel-width proxy.

4. Illustrative feasibility

The pilot compares a for-sale duplex houseplex, rental duplex houseplex, and four-unit rental rowhouse cluster—all permitted housing types in UR1, UR2, and UR3. For-sale RLV includes sales and closing costs. Rental RLV capitalizes stabilized NOI before deducting development cost and required profit. Demolition is charged only where a mapped building footprint exists. Assessed land plus improvement value is a low-confidence acquisition benchmark, not an appraisal.

5. Permit activity

Permit records are matched by normalized parcel number to show where observed applications overlap—or fail to overlap—with modeled capacity and feasibility. The layer is a delivery-gap diagnostic. It does not measure completed homes, and many records lack reliable housing-unit counts.

 Limitations

Read before interpreting results

  • This is an independent portfolio case study, not an official Tacoma capacity estimate or entitlement determination.
  • Existing-use eligibility relies on assessor descriptions; 1,229 ambiguous nonresidential parcels are held for manual review.
  • The partially vacant category is a current-data proxy, not Pierce County's official 2020 Buildable Lands classification.
  • Capacity omits bonuses and some site-specific code exceptions.
  • Critical-area GIS is a generalized screening source, not a field delineation. Unmapped resources and site-specific buffers may change the result.
  • Public parcel-level utility-easement geometry was not available, so easements require title and utility review and are not inferred.
  • Physical fit does not establish frontage, fire access, utility capacity, parking, tree compliance, title, or environmental feasibility.
  • Assessed value is a low-confidence acquisition benchmark, not market value.
  • Sale prices use a small public evidence sample; construction, financing, and fee assumptions remain illustrative.
  • Permit unit counts are incomplete and completion dates are unavailable.
  • Housing applications are independent historical context, not validation or the next stage of a selected prototype financial screen.
