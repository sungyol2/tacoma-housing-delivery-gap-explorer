About this explorer

This independent portfolio project asks how the volume and mix of housing applications changed after Tacoma's Home in Tacoma regulations took effect.

Parcel zoning and mapped constraints provide spatial context. The app does not estimate causation, completed housing production, or parcel redevelopment probability.

 Methods

How to read the evidence

1. Policy comparison

Active classified housing applications in current UR geography are divided into a five-year pre-policy period (February 2020–January 2025), Home in Tacoma Year One (February 2025–January 2026), and a current partial period. The five-year totals are divided by five before comparison with Year One.

2. Housing application ETL

Residential and Commercial Accela building records are canonicalized by permit number and grouped into likely projects. Structured fields and descriptions classify ADUs, detached houses, duplexes, 3–6-unit houseplexes, rowhouses, courtyard/cottage clusters, and multiplexes. Repairs and nonhousing scopes are excluded.

3. Parcel and zoning context

Pierce County parcel parts are dissolved to normalized parcel IDs. All parcel–zoning intersections are measured; the dominant current UR zone is displayed while material split zones remain in the gross capacity calculation.

4. Mapped constraints

Parks, institutional uses, rights-of-way, utilities, and other clear noncandidate uses are excluded. Mapped 40%+ slopes, wetlands, biodiversity areas, special flood hazard areas, and protected-water buffers are unioned before measuring the largest contiguous residual area.

 Limitations

Read before interpreting results

  • The before/after comparison is descriptive. It does not isolate zoning effects from interest rates, construction costs, owner decisions, project lead time, or other market conditions.
  • Current UR zoning geography is applied retrospectively because historical parcel-level zoning geometry is unavailable.
  • Permit descriptions, unit fields, project relationships, and parcel lineage remain imperfect; the City benchmark is shown separately rather than treated as a replication target.
  • The map is limited to existing-use candidate parcels, while the headline policy comparison uses active applications across current UR geography. Map totals need not equal the headline.
  • Applications indicate development interest, not permits issued, construction starts, completions, or net housing production.
  • Gross legal capacity does not subtract existing units and is not an estimate of net added capacity.
  • Critical-area GIS is a generalized screen, not a site delineation. Public utility-easement geometry was unavailable.
