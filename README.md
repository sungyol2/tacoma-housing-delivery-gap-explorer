# Home in Tacoma Housing Application Explorer

A parcel-level look at how housing applications changed after Tacoma's middle-housing zoning reform.

**[Open the live explorer](https://sungyol2.github.io/tacoma-housing-delivery-gap-explorer/app/)**

## Background

Tacoma's Home in Tacoma regulations took effect on February 1, 2025. The reform replaced the city's former single-family zoning with three Urban Residential districts—UR-1, UR-2, and UR-3—and allowed a broader range of housing types.

This project examines the first complete year under the new regulations. It focuses on a practical question: did the number and composition of housing applications change after the reform, and what kinds of housing account for that change?

## Goal

The explorer was developed to:

- compare housing applications before and after the reform;
- distinguish permit applications from estimated development projects and proposed homes;
- identify changes in the mix and scale of proposed housing;
- show where first-year applications occurred within the new Urban Residential districts; and
- provide parcel-level zoning, existing-use, and mapped environmental context.

The analysis is descriptive. It does not attempt to prove that the zoning reform caused the observed changes or predict which parcels will be developed.

## Method

Public building-permit records were standardized to one record per permit number. Residential and commercial building workflows were screened for applications that create or legalize housing; cancelled, voided, repair-only, and non-housing records were excluded.

Applications were classified into common housing types using structured fields and project descriptions. Related permits were grouped into estimated projects using shared project references, parcel, date, and housing type. Proposed-unit counts came from source unit fields or clear descriptions in the permit record.

The first Home in Tacoma year—February 2025 through January 2026—is compared with the annual average of the five preceding February-to-January periods. Current UR-1, UR-2, and UR-3 parcel geography is used for both periods because comparable historical parcel-level zoning boundaries were not available.

The parcel maps add two forms of context:

- gross housing allowance under current zoning; and
- publicly mapped steep slopes, wetlands, biodiversity areas, flood hazards, and protected-water buffers.

These parcel screens are planning-level context, not site-specific entitlement or environmental determinations.

## Data sources

- **City of Tacoma Open Data:** zoning, Accela permit records, building footprints, steep slopes, wetlands, biodiversity areas, protected waters, streams, and flood-hazard mapping
- **Pierce County Assessor-Treasurer / Pierce County GIS:** parcel geometry and assessor attributes
- **OpenStreetMap and CARTO:** basemap

Source data are dynamic and subject to their respective terms. This is an independent project and is not produced, reviewed, or endorsed by the City of Tacoma or Pierce County.

## Main findings

Across today's three Urban Residential districts:

- housing applications increased from a pre-policy annual average of **177** to **231** in Year One;
- estimated projects increased more modestly, from **170** to **193**;
- proposed homes increased from **227** to **416**; and
- proposed homes per estimated project rose from **1.33** to **2.16**.

The distinction between applications, projects, and units is important. Year One did not show an unprecedented number of separate projects; the larger shift was toward proposals containing more homes.

The housing mix also changed. Accessory dwelling units remained the largest category, while duplex and townhouse proposals increased substantially. Proposed units in 7–20-unit buildings also increased from a small base, and detached single-family applications declined.

These findings describe applications, not permits issued, construction starts, completed homes, or net housing production.
