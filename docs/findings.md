# Current Findings

All findings are planning-level screening results for an independent portfolio case study.

## 1. Current zoning creates broad theoretical capacity, not a delivery forecast

The current UR1–UR3 zoning inventory contains 58,319 base parcels. After excluding protected/institutional uses and holding ambiguous nonresidential uses for review, 56,484 parcels remain in the current-use candidate scope. Their modeled baseline allowance totals 418,264 units, with a median of six units per parcel. This is a zoning allowance, not net added capacity; reliable existing-unit data are not yet available for subtraction.

## 2. Critical-area screening removes false-positive vacant land

Of 56,484 candidates, 11,070 intersect at least one mapped critical-area constraint. After removing the unioned mapped geometry, 3,553 have less than 5,000 square feet of contiguous residual area and are screened out; 7,517 retain enough residual area but require site review. ## 3. Prototype fit varies by missing-middle form

The for-sale and rental duplexes each pass on 50,672 candidate parcels (89.7%); the four-unit rental rowhouse passes on 42,331 (74.9%). The rowhouse screen is narrower because it requires four-unit capacity, 4,700 gross square feet, 5,000 square feet of residual site area, and a 40-foot parcel-width proxy. Passing does not establish surveyed frontage, setbacks, access, parking, utility capacity, trees, title, or an architectural envelope.

## 4. Existing-site conditions narrow the interpretation

The current assessor/building screen classifies 1,662 candidates as vacant, 11,884 as partially vacant proxies, and 42,938 as developed. The partially vacant class is a transparent proxy based on improvement share and mapped building coverage, not an official Buildable Lands designation.

## 5. Baseline financial results are predominantly weak under current low-confidence inputs

The for-sale duplex baseline includes 49,857 very weak, 772 weak, and 43 marginal results; none are moderate or strong. Only 43 parcels are within $50,000 of break-even or above.

The rental comparison is weaker under the current sourced proxies and illustrative costs. All 50,672 physical-fit rental duplex parcels and all 42,331 physical-fit four-unit rental rowhouse parcels classify as very weak at baseline, and neither produces a near/above-break-even parcel in the upside stress test. This says the selected rent/cost/value relationship does not support these new rental prototypes—not that no rental project can work, because costs, fees, financing, rent premiums, tax treatment, and acquisition inputs remain incompletely validated.

The reverse-solved baseline diagnostic makes the gap legible. Against the $1,971 monthly two-bedroom rent proxy, the median physical-fit parcel requires about $4,850 per duplex unit or $4,220 per four-unit rowhouse unit to cover the modeled non-land cost, target profit, and assessed-value acquisition benchmark at the selected cap rate. These are model-implied break-even rents, not market-rent estimates; their distance from the proxy identifies the cost/value stack as the reason for the uniformly weak classification.

## 6. The upside stress test demonstrates sensitivity, not a forecast

The corrected for-sale upside stress test produces 744 near/above-break-even parcels, but it simultaneously raises sale price 10%, lowers hard cost 10%, lowers financing 15%, and lowers required return 10%. By comparison, one-factor changes of plus or minus 5% in sale price or hard cost produce far smaller shifts. The result remains a stacked sensitivity, not expected market performance.

## 7. Housing applications are context, not model validation

The 2021–2026 activity layer contains 1,446 matched residential new-building or positive-unit alteration records on current UR candidate parcels, representing 1,128 unique parcels. Product-, tenure-, and policy-aligned validation is not currently possible. An earlier post-February 1, 2025 four-unit rowhouse/townhouse-like diagnostic retained only four unique current-scope physical-fit parcels, and it does not validate either duplex model. Applications therefore remain separate from the development screening funnel.
